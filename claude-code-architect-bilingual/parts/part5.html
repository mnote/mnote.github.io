"Architecture is the art of arranging elements so that the whole has properties none of the elements possess. Without arrangement, there is only inventory." — Christopher Alexander, paraphrased
"所谓架构,就是把元素安排成一种形态,使整体具有任何单一元素都不具备的性质。没有安排,就只剩一份清单。" —— 克里斯托弗·亚历山大(意译)
Up to this point in the book, the three pillars have been treated separately. Skills were the subject of Part Two, subagents the subject of Part Three, hooks the subject of Part Four. Each part developed its pillar in depth, with mechanics, design patterns, and a working library installed by the chapter's end. The treatment was deliberately separate, because each pillar carries its own design discipline and learning each one in isolation is the only way to develop the discipline cleanly. But the separation is now over. The remainder of the book treats the three pillars as components of a single architecture, and this chapter is where the architecture itself gets explicit attention.
到本书此前的位置为止,三根支柱是分开讲的。Skill 是第二部分的主题,subagent 是第三部分的主题,hook 是第四部分的主题。每一部分都把各自那根支柱讲得很深——含机制、设计模式,以及"到章末装好的一套能跑起来的库"。这种分开是有意为之——因为每根支柱都自有一套设计纪律,而"一门门分开学",是把纪律练得干净的唯一办法。但"分开"这件事,到此为止。书剩下的部分,会把三根支柱当作一套架构里的若干组件来对待;而本章,正是让这套架构本身"被显式注意"的地方。
The argument of this chapter is that any sophisticated Claude Code environment uses all three pillars, that the pillars have a natural division of responsibility, that the composition between them follows recognizable patterns, and that the resulting environment has properties that no single pillar could produce on its own. The architecture is not arbitrary. It reflects the structure of the tool, the structure of real engineering work, and the structure of the failure modes that the architecture is built to prevent. Once the reader has internalized the architecture, the design of any new Claude Code environment becomes a matter of applying the same patterns to her specific work, with the patterns themselves as a stable starting point.
本章的论点是:任何一套"够复杂"的 Claude Code 环境,都会同时用上三根支柱;三根支柱之间存在一种自然的职责划分;它们彼此组合时,会遵循若干可识别的模式;而组合出来的环境,具有"任一根支柱单兵作战"都达不到的性质。这套架构并非随意——它反映的是工具本身的结构、真实工程工作的结构、以及"这套架构要去防住"的那几类失效模式的结构。一旦读者把架构内化,设计任何新的 Claude Code 环境,就变成"把这套同样的模式套到自己的具体工作上";模式本身,则成为一个稳定的起点。
Begin with the division of responsibility. Each pillar handles a class of problem that the other two cannot handle as well. Skills handle capability; they teach Claude how to do specific kinds of work consistently. Subagents handle specialization and parallelism; they give specific kinds of work their own focused context, their own tuned role, and the option to run alongside other work simultaneously. Hooks handle determinism; they impose mechanical guarantees on the lifecycle, ensuring that what must happen does happen, and what must not happen cannot happen. The three together cover capability, specialization, and boundary, which are the three categories of concern in any serious workflow.
从"职责划分"讲起。每一根支柱,各自处理一类"另两根处理得没那么好"的问题。Skill 处理"能力"——教 Claude 如何把某类工作一致地做对。Subagent 处理"专门化"与"并行"——为某类工作配上聚焦的上下文、调好的角色,以及"和其他工作并行跑"的可能性。Hook 处理"确定性"——对生命周期施加机械性的保证,让"必须发生的"确实发生,"绝不能发生的"绝不能发生。三者合起来,覆盖了"能力 / 专门化 / 边界"这三类——它们正是任何严肃工作流都绕不开的三个关切类别。

A simple way to remember the division is by the question each pillar answers. Skills answer the question, how should this be done. Subagents answer the question, who should do this. Hooks answer the question, what is permitted. A workflow that uses only skills can teach Claude to do good work but has no way to isolate different kinds of work and no way to guarantee enforcement. A workflow that uses only subagents has specialization but no stored knowledge and no enforcement. A workflow that uses only hooks has enforcement but no capability and no specialization. Each pillar, alone, is a partial answer. All three, together, form a complete one.
记住三者分工的一个简单办法,就是看每一柱回答的是哪个问题。Skills 回答的是"这件事该怎么做"。Subagents 回答的是"这件事该由谁做"。Hooks 回答的是"这件事什么被允许做"。只装 skills 的工作流,能教会 Claude 把活做漂亮,但既无法把不同类的工作隔离开来,也无法保证规约被执行。只装 subagents 的工作流,有专门化但没有沉淀下来的知识,也没有强制力。只装 hooks 的工作流,有强制力但没有能力,也没有专门化。任一柱单独存在,都只是一个"局部解";三柱一起,才构成一个完整的答案。

Two Layers of the Architecture · 架构的两层

The next observation, which is more subtle, is that the three pillars sit at different layers of the architecture. Skills and subagents both operate inside the agent loop. They are read by the model. They shape the model's behavior through prose. They live in the probabilistic space where the model interprets, reasons, and decides. Hooks operate outside the loop. They run as shell commands, not as language. They are not interpreted by the model. They are mechanical. The two layers, probabilistic inside and deterministic outside, are the natural partitioning of responsibility in a well-designed environment. The model is given freedom and capability inside the loop. The boundaries of the loop are deterministic. This shape, what one practitioner has called the deterministic outer and the probabilistic inner, is the central architectural pattern of the book, and it is worth holding in working memory because it explains almost every design decision in any complete environment.
下一条观察更微妙一些:三柱其实处在架构的不同层面上。Skills 和 subagents 都是在 agent loop 内部工作的——它们由模型读取,通过对模型吐出的文字(prose)塑形;它们栖身在那个"模型在其中解读、推理、做决定"的概率性空间里。Hooks 则是在 loop 外部工作的——它们以 shell 命令的形式运行,不是语言,也不会被模型"解读";它们是机械的。这两层——内部概率性、外部确定性——正是一个设计良好的环境里"职责"最自然的切分方式。模型在 loop 内被给予自由和能力;loop 的边界则是确定性的。这个形状(有位实践者称之为"外层确定、内层概率")是本书的中心架构范式,值得放在工作记忆里——因为它能解释任何一个完整环境里几乎所有的设计决策。
A worked illustration brings the architecture to life. Consider a developer who needs to perform a code review. The work has several components. The reviewer needs to know how good code reviews are written in this codebase. The reviewer needs to apply different kinds of attention to different aspects of the change. The reviewer needs to be prevented from accidentally approving changes that touch protected paths. Each of these components maps cleanly to one of the three pillars. The how is encoded in skills: a project-level pr-review skill that knows the team's preferred review format. The who is encoded in subagents: parallel security, performance, and architecture specialists who each handle their dimension.
举一个落地的例子,可以让这套架构活起来。想象一位开发者要执行一次 code review。这项工作包含好几个组件:reviewer 需要知道"在这个代码库里,一份好的 code review 应当怎么写";reviewer 需要对变更的不同方面施加不同种类的注意力;reviewer 还需要被防止在"被改的文件刚好落在受保护路径上"时不小心点了 approve。这每一个组件,都能干净地映射到三柱的其中一柱上。"怎么做"编码在 skill 里:一个 project 级别的 pr-review skill,知道本团队偏好的评审格式。"由谁做"编码在 subagent 里:并行运行的安全、性能、架构专员,各自处理自己那一维。

The what-is-permitted is encoded in hooks: a gate on PreToolUse that blocks any tool call that would mark a PR approved without explicit human confirmation when the PR touches authentication code.
"什么被允许"编码在 hooks 里:一道挂在 PreToolUse 上的 gate,会在"PR 触及身份认证相关代码、且没有任何人工确认时"拦截任何把它标为 approved 的工具调用。
The composition runs naturally. The developer asks for a review. Skills fire because the request matches their descriptions. Subagents are dispatched in parallel because the orchestrator pattern recognizes the multidimensional shape of the work. Hooks watch the lifecycle silently. The review unfolds in two minutes, the unified result is presented to the developer, and the deterministic guarantees protect the boundary that the model alone could not be trusted to respect. None of this required the developer to think about the architecture during the request. She typed a sentence. The architecture did the work.
三柱的组合自然地跑起来。开发者请求一次评审;skill 因为请求匹配了 description 而触发;subagent 因为 orchestrator 范式识别出了"这件事是多个维度"而被并行派出去;hooks 在生命周期里静默地守着。两分钟后,review 跑完,统一的结果呈到开发者面前,确定性的那条边界——模型本身无法被信赖会去守的那条——被 hooks 守住了。整件事,并不要求开发者在请求时心里还惦记着这套架构。她只敲了一句话,架构就替她把活干了。
A second observation about the architecture concerns the order of construction. The natural order in which a developer should build her environment is the order in which the pillars were taught in this book: skills first, subagents second, hooks third. The order is not arbitrary. Skills are the broadest and most generally useful pillar; they pay back immediately in almost every session, and they are the easiest to write well on the first attempt. Subagents are the next layer; they require slightly more design effort but produce dramatic benefits when the work has the right shape. Hooks are the most specialized; their value is concentrated in specific safety-critical concerns and in workflow enforcement, and they require the most careful design to install correctly. Building in this order means that the early investment produces immediate benefits, the middle investment produces architectural benefits, and the late investment produces the deterministic guarantees that complete the system. A developer who tries to build in reverse order, hooks first and skills last, often finds the experience frustrating, because hooks without skills and subagents are just constraints on a vanilla environment, and the constraints feel restrictive rather than enabling.
关于架构的第二条观察,是关于"建造顺序"的。开发者搭建她环境时,自然的顺序,就是本书讲述这三柱的顺序:先 skills,再 subagents,最后 hooks。这个顺序不是随意的。Skills 是覆盖面最广、最普适的那一柱;它几乎在每一个 session 里都能立刻见回报,而且第一次写就写得不错的难度也最低。Subagents 是下一层;它需要稍微多花点设计力气,但只要工作形状对得上,带来的收益是戏剧性的。Hooks 则是最专门化的;它的价值集中在某些"安全攸关"的具体关切,以及工作流强制,装的时候也需要最仔细的设计。按这个顺序搭,意味着前期的投入立刻就见到效益,中期的投入换来架构层面的效益,后期的投入换来"让系统真正闭环"的那些确定性保证。要是反过来——先装 hooks,最后才装 skills——体验常常很令人沮丧:没有 skills 和 subagents 的 hooks,只是套在一个"素面"环境上的约束,这种约束会让人感到被束缚,而不是被赋能。

A fourth observation concerns the design process for adding new capabilities to the environment. When the developer notices a friction in her work that she wants to address, she should ask herself which pillar fits. The classification questions are simple. Is this a knowledge gap, where the developer wishes the environment knew something specific? That is a skill. Is this an attention gap, where the developer wishes a different kind of attention were applied to a kind of work? That is a subagent. Is this a guarantee gap, where the developer wishes that something would always or never happen? That is a hook. Asking the right question first prevents the most common design mistakes, which are building skills that should have been hooks, building hooks that should have been skills, and building subagents that should have been narrow skills inside a regular session. The classification is fast, the right answer is usually obvious once the classification has been made, and the cumulative effect of asking the question every time is an environment whose components live in the right places.
第四条观察,是关于"向环境里加新能力"的设计流程。当开发者在工作中留意到一处她想去消除的摩擦时,她应当先问自己"这该落到哪一柱上"。分类问题本身很简单:这是一个"知识缺口"吗——也就是你希望环境知道某件具体的事?那就是 skill。这是一个"注意力缺口"吗——也就是你希望"某类工作被施加一种不同种类的注意力"?那就是 subagent。这是一个"保证缺口"吗——也就是你希望某件事要么永远发生、要么永远不发生?那就是 hook。先问对问题,可以规避最常见的设计错误:把本该做成 hook 的事做成 skill,把本该做成 skill 的事做成 hook,把本该是"普通 session 里一个窄 skill"的事做成 subagent。分类很快,一旦分类完成,正确答案通常就一目了然;而每次都问一遍这个问题,长年累月下来,环境里的每一个组件都会落在该落的位置上。
A fifth observation concerns over-engineering. The temptation to build an elaborate environment is real, and most developers go through a phase where they install too many skills, too many subagents, too many hooks, and the environment becomes harder to maintain than the work it was supposed to support. The remedy is to start small, add components only when their need is concrete, and remove components that have not been used in a few months. A small, well-designed environment of fifteen skills, six subagents, and eight hooks is dramatically more useful than a sprawling one with fifty of each. A sixth observation concerns the maintenance posture of the environment. A well-built environment is not a static artifact. It evolves with the developer's work. New skills get added when new frictions emerge. Old skills get refined as the developer's preferences change. Subagents get reshaped as the developer learns which roles benefit most from isolation. Hooks get updated as new threats and conventions emerge. The .claude directory of any active developer should look noticeably different at the end of a year from how it looked at the start, because the developer's environment has been growing alongside her work. The maintenance investment is small; thirty minutes per week is sufficient to keep an environment current and useful. The compounding of that investment, across the years a developer uses her environment, is what produces the gap between developers who treat their tooling seriously and developers who do not.

What This Architecture Means · 这套架构意味着什么

The final observation in this chapter is the one that matters most for the rest of the book. The composition principle, repeated throughout these chapters, is not just a design pattern. It is the difference between using Claude Code and engineering with it. A developer who has built a thoughtful three-pillar environment is doing something fundamentally different from a developer who has not. She is operating inside an environment that has been shaped to her work, that absorbs frictions she would otherwise carry, that protects her from mistakes she would otherwise make, and that produces a level of consistency and quality across her output that no amount of careful prompting alone could match. The investment to build this environment is real but bounded. The benefit, once built, compounds for as long as the developer continues to work in the environment. There is no comparable investment available to a software engineer in 2026 that produces anything close to the leverage of a well-designed Claude Code environment, because no other tool sits at the intersection of capability, specialization, and enforcement that Claude Code does.
本章的最后一条观察,是往后整本书里最关键的一条。在前面这些章节里反复出现的"组合原则",不只是一个设计范式——它是"用 Claude Code"和"用 Claude Code 做工程"之间那条分水岭。一个搭建了深思熟虑的"三柱环境"的开发者,和一个没有这样做的开发者,做着本质不同的事。前者是在一个已经被塑造得贴合她工作的环境里运转:环境吸收她原本要自己扛的摩擦、保护她免于她原本会犯的错、并让她产出的一致性和质量达到"再仔细的 prompt 工程"也匹配不上的水平。搭建这个环境的投入是真实但有限的;一旦搭好,收益会在她继续使用环境的每一天里不断复利。在 2026 年,软件工程师能做的投资里,没有哪一项能在杠杆效率上接近"一个设计良好的 Claude Code 环境"——因为没有任何别的工具,同时坐在能力、专门化、强制这三件事的交汇点上,而 Claude Code 坐在那里。

The first pattern is what might be called the project-skill, user-subagent split. In well-designed multi-developer environments, project-specific knowledge tends to live in skills at the project level, while role specialists tend to live as subagents at the user level. The reason is that knowledge is project-specific (a Python project has different conventions from a TypeScript project) but roles are usually personal (a developer's preferred way of approaching code review does not change much when she switches projects). The split allows the developer to maintain a stable personal subagent library that she carries from project to project, while each project contributes its own skills that augment her subagents with project-specific knowledge. The composition pattern from Chapter Fourteen depends on this split working cleanly, and developers who set up their environments with the split in mind from the start avoid a common refactoring later when they realize their subagents have accumulated project-specific knowledge that should have lived elsewhere.

A final architectural observation, before the case studies begin, concerns the relationship between the three-pillar architecture and the deeper craft of software engineering. # Blog Post Outline

When asked for a blog post, structure it as follows.

1. **Hook (one paragraph)**: Open with a specific, concrete observation or question. Avoid abstract framing. The hook should make a technical reader want to keep reading.
2. **The problem (one or two paragraphs)**: Describe the actual pain the post will address, in technical terms. Use a specific example. Do not pretend the problem is more general than it is.
3. **The mental model or framework (two or three paragraphs)**: Lay out the way of thinking about the problem that the post is going to argue for. This is the substantive contribution.
4. **The application (one or two examples)**: Show the framework applied to one or two concrete cases. Use real code or real configuration. Do not abstract too far.

6. **The takeaway (one or two sentences)**: A single concrete statement of what the reader should do or remember.

A fourth reflection concerns the limits of what this kind of environment can do. The architecture does not make the founder smarter. It does not produce ideas she would not have produced. It does not make strategic decisions for her. The architecture absorbs the conventions, the formats, and the role-shaping that her work requires, leaving her attention free for the work that actually requires her judgment. The strategic decisions, the brand voice that gives the marketing its character, the product instincts that decide which feature to build next, all of those remain hers. The environment is a tool, and like all tools, it amplifies what is already there rather than replacing it. A founder with poor strategic instincts will not become a great strategist by installing a Claude Code environment. A founder with strong instincts will find that her instincts now reach further, because the surrounding labor of executing on them is partly absorbed by the environment.

A sixth and final reflection concerns the broader implication of multi-domain Claude Code environments for the way solo founders and small teams operate in the present moment. The combination of cheap intelligence and well-engineered environments has shifted the boundary of what a single person can sustain operationally. Companies that would have required a team of five a decade ago can now be run by a single founder with a thoughtful Claude Code environment, because the environment absorbs the operational labor that used to require additional headcount. The leverage is real and substantial. Founders who recognize this shift and invest in their tooling accordingly are operating with a structural advantage over founders who treat their tools as commodities. The architecture this book has been teaching is, in this sense, a piece of strategic infrastructure for solo and small-team operators in the current era, and the case study above is meant to demonstrate not merely a productivity gain but a different kind of company that becomes possible to run when the architecture is in place.
